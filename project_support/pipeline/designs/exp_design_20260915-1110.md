## 3. Analysis And Direction
Source: M196 boundary_assignment.py, boundary_loss:
boundary = unique(edge[gt[edge[:,0]] != gt[edge[:,1]]]).
Let E_x={(i,j) in E:y_i!=y_j}, B=endpoints(E_x), and H=G[B].
For every i in B there exists j in B with (i,j) in E_x, hence in H.
Each connected component C of H contains at least one opposite-label edge.
Consequently 0<sum_C y<|C|. This also holds for the crossing-only graph.
The 0808 example C1={0,1}, C2={2,3}, y=(1,1,0,0), with within-component
edges only has B=empty, not two homogeneous boundary components.
Thus its zero-gradient homogeneous-component rationale does not apply to
either induced-boundary or crossing-only components. It remains a valid
example for unrestricted full-scene groups, not BCA's boundary domain.

The other algebraic concern remains: L_comp=(sum_C logZ_kC(z_C)-y.dot(z))/n.
dL/dz_i=(conditional inclusion probability_i-y_i)/n.
Adding an independent scalar to each component's logits leaves this loss
unchanged. This removes component count calibration, but does not prove
harm: BCA also lacks global count calibration and retained BCE can supply it.
Mixed disconnected components can exist, so grouping is not generally equal
to full-boundary BCA. Do not upgrade this distinction to novelty or efficacy.
Complexity remains O(sum_C |C| min(k_C,|C|-k_C)) plus graph traversal.
No new optimizer, coefficient, or production computation is proposed.

Prior-art lead from durable evidence: Tarlow et al. 2012, arXiv:1210.4899.
