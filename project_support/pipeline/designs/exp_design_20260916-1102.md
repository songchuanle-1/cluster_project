## 3. Analysis & Direction
### 3.1 Corrected diagnosis
The native positive set already agrees with the mask 95.0% of the time (783 of 824
slots). The earlier "positives never require mask agreement" story is retracted.

The measured failure is discrimination: 34 of 123 argmax selections (27.6%) are
queries that were never marked as referent at training time, and success is 5.9%
when that happens versus 93.3% when the selection is a positive. Failure
decomposition: 32/38 = "top-scoring query was never a referent positive", 6/38 =
"positive whose own mask is wrong".

### 3.2 Why this is hard to fix inside the current constraints
About 7 queries per expression are all labelled referent, so the ordering within the
positive set is unconstrained by the referent loss, and 2455 mask-OK duplicate
hypotheses are labelled no-object. The canonical fixes — one-to-one/Hungarian
matching, set-based losses, mask-aware positives — are RefMask3D and DETR prior art
and sit in the banned assignment-successor family. Readout-only repairs are measured
to fail (23 variants plus two probes), region scoring is measured to fail, and the
multi-scale axis is decision-inert.

### 3.3 Ceiling for prioritisation
Restricting selection perfectly to training positives would take TRAIN success from
69.1% to roughly 92-95% (conservative, extrapolation-free floor: 113/123 = 91.9%
expressions contain at least one mask-OK positive). Referent selection is confirmed
as the highest-value target; this is an oracle bound for prioritisation only, never a
deployable result.

independent samples; the ceiling numbers are TRAIN diagnostics, not mIoU, and the
oracle bound assumes the per-positive mask-OK rate carries over.

The larger risk is unchanged and now better quantified: the measured defect has a
known prior-art fix, and the fixes that remain inside the bans are the ones already
measured to fail. Continuing to probe without a scope decision risks producing more
negative diagnostics instead of a trainable variant.
