## 3. Analysis & Direction
### 3.1 The measured asymmetry
On the same 33 recoverable failures, the raw paired 2D modality ranks the referred
instance's nearest-neighbour position better than the final 3D representation:
top-3 72.7% vs 48.5%, top-5 81.8% vs 57.6%, median rank 2 vs 4; paired, 2D is better
in 20 of 33 and worse in 7. The model already fuses this modality before the
encoder, so the fused representation is losing identity content that the input has.

### 3.2 Where the contribution could and could not be
- Could be: a mechanism that carries instance identity from the paired modality to
  the referent decision through a path that does not have to survive the encoder's
  category-forming transformations, plus a derivation of why that path is not just
  another fusion.
- Could not be: raising the 2D branch weight, adding a second fusion of the same two
  feature sets, a modality gate, or a modality-attention selector. Those are numeric
  or recombination changes; MDIN-style dual-branch modality fusion is prior art.

### 3.3 Constraints the design must respect
~0.24 G FLOPs headroom inside the <=10 G historical cap; no N^2 attention; no new
dataset or annotation; seed1999 and the existing schedule unchanged; no test-set
selection; must report the existing 85/33/5 strata with scene-level uncertainty.

## 7. Risk Assessment
Centred cosine across different ambient dimensionalities is comparable only in rank
terms. Instance centroids are coarse; 33 failures over 32 scenes are not independent.
The measurement is similarity structure, not phrase alignment, and a modality can be
identity-rich while poorly aligned to the expression. All numbers are TRAIN
diagnostics, not mIoU.
